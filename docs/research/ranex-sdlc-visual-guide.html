Plain-language explainer · POL-SDLC-001

How Ranex turns an idea into a trusted outcome

One path for product work, defects, security fixes, operations, and Hermes upstream changes. Risk changes how much proof is required—not whether proof is required.

Target model—not a claim of current implementation

The policy is ACCEPTED. Implementation maturity remains R_AND_D until adoption gates SDLC-ADOPT-A through SDLC-ADOPT-E pass. This page explains the model; the normative policy and control catalog win if wording differs.

Every production behavior must trace back to an owned need and forward to verification, release, health, and outcome evidence.

At a glance

Five phases, thirteen normal-flow states, three exception states

The friendly phase names help humans navigate. The monospace names are canonical WorkItemStatus values. The five phases show the thirteen-state normal path; BLOCKED, CANCELLED, and ROLLED_BACK remain first-class governed exception states.

It is iterative, not a one-way waterfall.

New evidence can return work to discovery, definition, design, or implementation. The reason, authority, affected baseline, stale evidence, and next owner are recorded. History is never erased.

Outcome loop

Are we solving the right problem?

Govern → shape → discover → review the outcome.

Delivery loop

Did we build and ship it safely?

Specify → design → plan → build → verify → release.

Reliability loop

Is it healthy, and what improves next?

Observe → respond → recover → learn → improve.

All three loops share one work-item ID and one evidence graph. An agent run, pull request, deployment, or incident links to the work item; none replaces it.

When evidence says “go back”

Verification fails

Return to IN_PROGRESS, DESIGN, or DEFINITION—whichever assumption failed.

Rollout becomes unsafe

RELEASING → ROLLED_BACK → TRIAGE. Verify the safe state and create a linked new attempt.

The outcome disappoints

OUTCOME_REVIEW → DISCOVERY or DEFINITION. Change the hypothesis or contract.

Work cannot continue

Any active state may become BLOCKED with a reason, owner, time, and next decision.

Pre-release work stops

Any pre-release state may become CANCELLED with impact, cleanup, and retained evidence.

Inputs change

Mark dependent evidence stale, perform impact analysis, then re-verify the exact new subject.

Linked lifecycles—not extra work-item states

IncidentStatus

Detect → acknowledge → mitigate → verify recovery → review → track actions → create linked improvement work.

Maintenance

A defect, vulnerability, dependency, debt, or service signal creates a linked work item that follows the normal lifecycle.

Retirement

Plan consumer/data/access exit → migrate and revoke → archive and tear down → audit the retired capability.

Proportionate assurance

One process, four risk lanes

Policy derives the lane from facts and effects. A maker may ask for more assurance but cannot lower the lane. Select a lane to highlight it.

Standard

Small and reversible.

Peer review, focused deterministic checks, and a standard reversible release.

Enhanced

User-visible or persistent.

Full requirements/design, independent V&V, broader tests, and release/rollback plans.

Critical

Trust or irreversible impact.

Threat/data/ops analysis, ADR/RFC, specialist challenge, recovery proof, and explicit human permit.

Emergency

Active harm.

Smallest safe mitigation, incident authority, targeted checks, time limit, and mandatory normalization.

An exception cannot rewrite reality.

It may acknowledge missing proof and accept bounded risk. It cannot turn missing or failing evidence into PASS.

Exact-subject proof

Evidence follows the change

Every link is durable, queryable, and bound to the exact subject it proves. If a material input changes, dependent evidence becomes stale and must be impact-analyzed and regenerated.

  2. Desired outcome + measure
  3. Requirement + acceptance example
  4. Design + risk controls
  5. Delivery slice + exact commit
  6. Review + test + security proof
  7. Immutable artifact + deployment
  8. Health + support + outcome
  9. Learning + follow-up

Chat is working material, not sufficient evidence.

No required link may exist only in a model conversation. The released artifact must be the verified artifact.

Decision separation

Who decides what

A small team may combine compatible roles. Enhanced and critical work still separates the maker, independent acceptance, risk authority, and release authority.

Humans and AI agents

Research, refine, propose, build, test, review, and report evidence inside a bounded role.

Deterministic gates

Evaluate machine-verifiable facts against the exact candidate. A failing fact remains failed.

Named human owners

Accept value, requirements, architecture, operational fitness, security/data risk, and critical exceptions.

Independent reviewer / V&V

Fresh evaluation without editing the subject or borrowing the maker’s authority.

Release operator

Stages, observes, halts, or rolls back only under an exact permit.

Incident commander

Coordinates impact, communication, mitigation, recovery, and linked improvement work.

AI is execution capacity, not accountability.

An agent cannot own the outcome, lower risk, waive controls, approve its own missing evidence, grant itself a permit, merge, release, or close work.

L0–L12 and AI-G0–AI-G10 belong to the governed AI execution subprocess. They are not canonical work-item states, and a successful run never auto-advances WorkItemStatus.

Safe cutover and extensions

Exactly one system is the canonical writer in every mode—even during transitional dual run. External extensions/plugins use ExtensionStatus, first-party modules use ModuleStatus, and provider/model transports use RouteStatus. Qualification supplies immutable evidence but cannot change any of those states. MCP tools use the applicable module or extension lifecycle.

Safe self-development: N controls N+1

An immutable Ranex release N governs candidate N+1. The candidate cannot modify or restart the controller, approve itself, issue its permit, or install itself. Independent review, deterministic gates, a human release permit, rollback drill, observation, and outcome review come first.

Hard rules

  • No blind upstream merge.
  • Retain upstream history and attribution.
  • Every inherited surface has an owner and compatibility proof.
  • Promote immutable approved artifacts.
  • No mutable Git plugin update in target mode.
  • No environment-presence route auto-selection.
  • No user last-writer-wins authority override.
  • No dual authority during migration.

BuiltImplementation, developer tests, and docs are complete.
VerifiedThe exact candidate satisfies acceptance and quality proof.
ReleasedThe verified artifact reached users through an authorized, reversible path.
OperatedHealth, support, recovery, and ownership are proven.
Outcome reviewedActual results were compared with the intended outcome.
ClosedRecords, temporary controls, decisions, and follow-ups are reconciled.
MERGED is an event, not a lifecycle state.

Merge means code landed. It does not mean released, healthy, useful, or closed.

Evidence-based improvement

Assess each capability. Improve the weakest high-risk constraint.

Ranex does not use one reassuring maturity percentage. Each capability keeps its ordinal level, effectiveness, coverage, and confidence separate so strong flow cannot hide missing authority, stale proof, or unsafe recovery.

Honest current baseline: not assessed

The policy and rubric now exist, but no evidence-bound capability assessment has been approved. Documentation alone could support at most 1 — DEFINED; it does not prove operation.

NOT_ASSESSED

Report four facts—never one blended score

Capability rating

Result is NOT_ASSESSED, UNKNOWN, NOT_APPLICABLE, or SCORED. Only SCORED has level 0–4.

Effectiveness

UNKNOWN, REGRESSING, MIXED, or MEETS_TARGET.

Coverage

Included ÷ eligible work from one snapshot, jointly split by work class and risk lane with reconciled exclusions.

Confidence

LOW, MEDIUM, or HIGH. High needs every adequacy check plus independent sign-off.

Averages cannot erase a control failure.

The 0–4 values are ordered labels, not quantities to add or average. A wrong-subject pass, maker self-approval, missing permit, expired authority, or unverified critical rollback remains red regardless of every other result. Ratings never authorize a transition or rank people.

The evidence-anchored 0–4 capability scale

0 · ABSENT Required control is missing or unsafe

Contain risk; name an owner; define the minimum contract, event, and rejection route.

1 · DEFINED The rule exists

Versioned purpose, owner, scope, entry and exit, evidence, authority, failure route, and metric definitions exist. Documents alone stop here.

2 · OPERATED Real work demonstrates it

Real evidence includes a normal path and at least one rejection, invalidation, exception, or backward path actually taken.

3 · CONTROLLED Operation and failures are visible

A qualified measurement system exposes distributions, misses, false passes, exceptions, and responses.

4 · IMPROVING Measured benefit clears uncertainty

Benefit persists across 2+ windows, clears local measurement noise, and does not harm guardrails.

When result is not SCORED, there is no level. NOT_ASSESSED means no review happened; UNKNOWN means trustworthy evidence or applicability is unresolved. Neither is zero or pass. A level needs enacted practice, durable evidence, and outcome/guardrail proof appropriate to it; documents alone stop at level 1.

Which capability should improve first?

P0 · CONTROL NOW

Active harm or a non-tailorable invariant, truth, evidence, authority, privacy/security, or recovery breach. Stop or contain and restore control.

P1 · IMPROVE NEXT

Result NOT_ASSESSED/UNKNOWN, level 0–1, overdue critical action, repeated escape, high-exposure blockage, or LOW-confidence instrumentation need.

P2 · IMPROVE DELIBERATELY

Absent P0/P1: level 2, unproven or unhealthy effectiveness, material flow/outcome harm, or another missing P3 condition.

P3 · SUSTAIN

Absent P0–P2: level 3–4, MEETS_TARGET, reconciled coverage, healthy guardrails, no adverse trend, and confidence above LOW.

Evaluate P0 → P1 → P2 → P3; the first match wins. Within a tier, sort by consequence, exposure, recurrence, downstream blocking, then capability gap. A valid all-N/A assessment has no tier.

What to gauge—and the first practical improvement

Assess a named service or value stream over a declared window. A domain is SCORED only after every applicable vital control is scored; its level is the lowest supported level among them—never an average. Every registry tuple binds an immutable same-scope assessment, so an assessor cannot omit the weak control. Open a group to inspect its diagnostic signals.

Three anti-gaming checks are mandatory.

NOT_APPLICABLE needs a predeclared rule/query and approval proving no eligible work or qualifying trigger; ambiguity is UNKNOWN. One snapshot must reconcile totals, joint work-class/risk-lane strata, exclusions, and typed adverse queries. A result cannot be SCORED with an unresolved material gap or unless every applicable adverse category includes every eligible item with zero exclusions. HIGH confidence requires sample, duration, representativeness, authenticity, freshness, missingness, and data-quality tests to pass plus independent sign-off.

How improvement moves

1. Scope

Name capability, service, lanes, rubric, and time window.

2. Collect

Include raw evidence from success, failure, exception, and rollback.

3. Rate

Report rating result/level, effectiveness, coverage, and confidence separately.

4. Prioritize

Apply P0–P3. Hard-control findings always win.

5. Experiment

Freeze comparator and decision rule; change one constraint; measure with guardrails.

6. Recheck

After the declared window, retain, scale, simplify, change, or revert.

Measure before judging.

Define the goal, question, formula, population, decision, and owner. One immutable design binds the metric and qualified harness ID/version/configuration. Never assume zero noise: a zero floor needs method evidence and independent approval; uncertainty N/A needs the exact approved deterministic-measure rule. Count infrastructure errors separately. A below-noise result is inconclusive—not improvement.

Improvement is proven effectiveness—not an action-item count.

Every experiment names a causal stage/control, evidence, hypothesis, owner, comparator, measure, counter-metrics, decision rule, review date, and revert rule. Use the control assessment and domain projection templates; the core policy and control catalog are normative.
